Nepal

Nepal, is a landlocked country in South Asia. It is mainly situated in the Himalayas, but also includes parts of the Indo-Gangetic Plain. It borders the Tibet Autonomous Region of China to the north, and India to the south, east, and west, while it is narrowly separated from Bangladesh by the Siliguri Corridor, and from Bhutan by the Indian state of Sikkim. Nepal has a diverse geography, including fertile plains, subalpine forested hills, and eight of the world's ten tallest mountains, including Mount Everest. Kathmandu is the nation's capital and the largest city.
The name "Nepal" is first recorded in texts from the Vedic period of the Indian subcontinent, the era in ancient Nepal when Hinduism was founded, the predominant religion of the country. In the middle of the first millennium BC, Gautama Buddha, the founder of Buddhism, was born in Lumbini in southern Nepal. Parts of northern Nepal were intertwined with the culture of Tibet. The centrally located Kathmandu Valley is intertwined with the culture of Indo-Aryans, and was the seat of the prosperous Newar confederacy known as Nepal Mandala. By the 18th century, the Gorkha Kingdom achieved the unification of Nepal.

Places To Visit

Nepal brings together high mountains, subtropical forests, lakes, and historic cities. Trek in the Himalayas, explore wildlife parks, or discover cultural centers such as Kathmandu, Pokhara, and Lumbini. Spring and autumn are popular seasons for travel.

Kathmandu

Kathmandu Valley blends a lively capital with historic squares, temples, and traditional neighborhoods. Its seven monument zones include important Hindu and Buddhist sites and are recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage property.

Pokhara

Framed by the Annapurna range, Pokhara pairs mountain views with a chain of lakes. Spend time by Phewa Lake, visit lakeside temples, or head out for boating, hiking and paragliding.

Lumbini

Lumbini is revered as the birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha. The sacred garden, Maya Devi Temple, Ashoka Pillar and ancient monastery remains make it a meaningful destination for pilgrims and history enthusiasts.

Chitwan

Explore the Terai’s forests, grasslands and rivers on a guided jungle safari or canoe trip. Chitwan National Park is known for its one-horned rhinos, Bengal tigers and rich birdlife, alongside the culture of the Tharu community.

Manang

Set in the rain shadow of the Annapurna range, Manang has a striking, dry high-mountain landscape. It is a highlight of the Annapurna trekking region, with trails, traditional villages and sweeping Himalayan views.

Shey Phoksundo

Remote Shey-Phoksundo National Park spans Dolpa and Mugu, protecting dramatic high-Himalayan and trans-Himalayan landscapes. Visit Phoksundo Lake, Dho and Ringmo villages, or explore mountain trails, monasteries and wildlife.

Things To Know Before Visiting Nepal

How To Travel?

Public buses are affordable, while domestic flights and private vehicles can save time. Check schedules and road conditions, especially on mountain routes.

Where To Stay?

Choose from hotels, guesthouses, and trekking lodges. Book ahead during busy seasons, and check the location and facilities before you reserve.

Where To Trek?

Choose a route that fits your time, fitness, and altitude experience. Check local conditions and guidance before heading into the mountains.

Cultures

Nepal’s culture blends Hindu and Buddhist traditions with many regional customs. Experience this diversity through festivals, temples, and everyday etiquette. At sacred sites, remove your shoes and walk clockwise around stupas.

Festivals

From Dashain and Tihar to local jatras, Nepal’s festivals bring communities together through family gatherings, music, rituals, and colorful celebrations throughout the year.

Temples

Visit sacred places such as Pashupatinath and Swayambhunath, where Hindu and Buddhist traditions meet. Remove your shoes, dress respectfully, and follow local customs.

Heritages

Explore historic squares, stupas, and palaces across Nepal. UNESCO-listed places such as Kathmandu Valley and Lumbini preserve centuries of architecture, art, and religious history.

Language

Nepali is Nepal’s official language, while many communities also speak local languages. English is common in tourism centers and trekking areas. Namaste is a polite greeting, and dhanyavad means “thank you.”

Nepali

Nepali is Nepal’s official language and is spoken across the country as a first or second language. It is written in Devanagari, and a greeting such as namaste is a friendly way to begin a conversation.

English

English is often used in tourism, education, and business, especially in larger towns and cities. It can be useful at hotels, travel offices, and popular trekking routes, though it may be less common in rural areas.

Hindi

Hindi is understood by some people in Nepal, particularly in places with close cross-border ties and through media and trade. It is not spoken everywhere, so a few Nepali words are still useful.
